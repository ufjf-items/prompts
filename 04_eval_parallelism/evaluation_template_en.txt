## Syntactic parallelism:
Syntactic parallelism between the options means making sure they all have a similar grammatical structure. It guarantees that the parts of the sentence are of the same form across the options (for example: same verb tense, same part of speech, same preposition).

### Example of a question that does not have syntactic parallelism:
According to the text, Sofia was very happy because
Options:
   a) she drew a garden full of flowers and butterflies.
   b) the teacher praised her drawing.
   c) she got a set of paints and brushes.
   d) she took part in a contest at school.
-> 3 options keep Sofia as the subject, and 1 option switches to a different, explicit subject (the teacher).

## Semantic parallelism:
The options must belong to the same field of meaning or thematic context addressed in the command or in the support text.

### Example of a question that does not have semantic parallelism:
If the stem speaks of happiness over something Sofia did or received, all of the options must sit within that context. When one or more of them stray from the theme, semantic parallelism is broken.

According to the text, Sofia was very happy because
Options:
   a) she got a set of paints and brushes.
   b) the teacher praised her drawing.
   c) she took part in a contest at school.
   d) it rained a lot over the weekend.
-> It speaks of the weather, with no relation to Sofia or to happiness tied to the context of what she did or received at school.
